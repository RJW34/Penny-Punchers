# Further iconic-move design space — not additions to the current roster by default

These eight ideas extend the brainstorming, not the 38-entry implementation catalog. They need a future archetype or a separate owner-approved system experiment. Their prices are illustrative, not approved.

## F-01 — Debt Collector

**Capture-to-combo throw · 900 credits · round_lease**

Reference: Command-throw conversion idea; requires separate grappler identity.

A capture creates a short, heavily prorated standing conversion, not raw damage. No recapture within the same combo and no capture from hit/blockstun. Unlike Clinch Entry, it needs a dedicated reset/combo contract.

Why separate: It would expand Rook into a different throw-conversion archetype before the basic throw and command-throw arbitration are verified.

Counterplay target: Preemptive jump/backspace or strike; clear capture range and whiff recovery.

## F-02 — High Roller

**Branching aerial approach · 600 credits · round_lease**

Reference: Cammy Hooligan-type approach family.

A committed visible leap can end in a strike, landing feint or close airborne capture, each with separate collision/recovery. No tracking to the target after the initial leap.

Why separate: The current roster needs normal aerial readability and minimum-height rules first; this is better on an aerial specialist.

Counterplay target: Anti-air the committed arc; stay outside grab eligibility; punish a feint landing.

## F-03 — Downward Draft

**Air projectile · 600 credits · round_lease**

Reference: Aerial projectile family; new original route, no claimed exact reference-game implementation.

One angled projectile from a forward jump above a minimum height; locks remaining air action and creates explicit landing recovery. No projectile plus dive-kick cancel by default.

Why separate: Adding this to every zoner/rushdown hybrid would erase distinct approach weaknesses.

Counterplay target: Parry, move under at the correct range, air-to-air or punish landing.

## F-04 — Collateral

**Orbiting follow-up objects · 1500 credits · super_activation**

Reference: Oro Tengu Stone / Rose Soul Satellite functions.

One paid finite state spawns deterministic nonrandom objects that follow only specifically listed attacks. No earned stock, no random bonus object and no extension from hits. Counts/lifetimes are object state, never an ammunition shop.

Why separate: Use a future object specialist; first finish the single-field interaction model.

Counterplay target: Deny initial activation, outlast the state, parry contacts or force the defined knockdown termination.

## F-05 — Double Entry

**Delayed echo follow-ups · 1500 credits · super_activation**

Reference: Rose Soul Illusion-type shadows.

A short state produces one predictable delayed echo of whitelisted normals; echoes cannot create new echoes or money. All contacts use a single guard-facing rule, existing scaling and visible delay.

Why separate: Owner/echo contact interaction and defense fairness require their own prototype; do not layer this on Overtime.

Counterplay target: Block/parry known rhythms, disengage, interrupt activation, or knock down the owner.

## F-06 — Demand Notice

**Held charged projectile · 1200 credits · super_activation**

Reference: Charged-super design space, not copied Denjin unblockable rules.

Debit at the beginning of vulnerable charge startup. Holding selects within a fixed min/max release interval; release creates a blockable projectile with explicit contact count and no separate stored charge. Interrupting charge does not refund. No neutral cancel that banks the charge.

Why separate: Potentially replaces, not supplements, a future selected super; test stall, charge-time value and parry behavior first.

Counterplay target: Interrupt, change spacing, jump at the measured release or parry/block.

## F-07 — Break Clause

**Guard cancel · 600 credits · direct_system_activation**

Reference: Broader fighting-game guard-cancel design experiment.

Only during actual blockstun, commit credits to an interruptible delayed push-away with zero damage. Never usable in hitstun, throw capture or as a round-start burst. Both actors return to measured spacing, not guaranteed advantage.

Why separate: This alters the defensive system rather than merely adding a character move. Requires a separate owner decision and cannot become mandatory to survive funded pressure.

Counterplay target: Bait and punish the cancel or take the returned space.

## F-08 — Cut Losses

**Contact-only movement cancel · 600 credits · direct_system_activation**

Reference: FADC-style cancel design space, not a copied Focus system.

Pay on a legal forward-dash cancel of a whitelisted noninvulnerable move after HIT only. No blocked invincible reversal bailout, no whiff cancel, no backwards escape or defensive invulnerability. Normal scaling/juggle persist.

Why separate: A universal cancel layer can overwhelm Third Strike-like commitment; it is an optional separate ruleset, not part of the 38-entry plan.

Counterplay target: Deny the initial hit; defend the later route according to real advantage.

## Deliberately rejected shortcuts

Do not add a health potion, passive +damage, wider parry window, cheaper-super coupon, interest account, loser-only character stat boost, an automatic combo macro, purchased invincible escape from hitstun, persistent equipment inheritance, random-rarity item shop, or a new resource merely called "economy meter."

Money-to-health can incentivize stalling and changes kill thresholds. Discount/income products compound retained funds. Automatic execution avoids the intended skill. Raw power doesn't fix a move with no tactical purpose. These are design risk judgments for this project, not claims that no game can ever use those concepts well.
