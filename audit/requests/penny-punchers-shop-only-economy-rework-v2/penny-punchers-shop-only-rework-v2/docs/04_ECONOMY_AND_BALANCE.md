# Prices, repeated EX and comeback tests
All numbers are proposed. Candidate starts600/cap3600/shop cap2400; win1200; loss1200/1200/1500; draw900; skill cap300. Removing the old initial 300 outcome bonus is an explicit candidate choice because performance bonuses can favor the stronger player. It is NOT a proof of comeback fairness. Preserve the historical payout as a lab control, not an active fallback during ordinary matches.

Settlement (each player): nominal result payout from OLD tier, nominal skill receipts; grant result payout first up to wallet space, then grant earned skill up to remaining space; retain separate cap clipping. Update tier after choosing the result amount (win -1 floor0, loss +1 cap2, draw unchanged). Half-point scoring and finite match remain. Duplicate settlement is idempotent; a changed winner/hash with same identity is rejected. No provisional result funds a cart.

With equal initial spending under candidate rules, an opening winner has no automatic result-payout bank lead. Its skill bonus can create at most300 more than the opponent in that round. This is not a total-lead bound: saved purchase money matters. Example: both buy600; win/lose with no skill both enter next shop1200. Winner earns300 versus loser's100 →1500/1300. If winner saved600 while loser bought600, add that600 difference. Test thresholds, not only average cash.

Repeatable EX radically changes the valuation. Test sustained projectile loops, repeat reversal, chip sequences, corner defense, confirms/cancels, command-throw mix, and one/two EX combinations. Paid moves cannot need another paid item to be countered in every ordinary situation. Keep free ordinary anti-air/parry/throw/movement. Record legitimate checkmates caused by prior mistakes separately from unavoidable systemic offense. Armor/field/installs already live must use reward provenance and category rules.

## Required experiments (after control/camera and policy repairs)
1. Full-match crossover: A historical payouts/no skill; B historical payouts/capped skill; C candidate equal initial payouts/capped skill; D same as C/no skill. A/B/C/D all SHOP-ONLY to isolate causes. Disable bonuses only in lab controls; final requested rules enable them.
2. Round-two same score 1–0 and same fighters/art selection, compare earned-bank differential vs equal-bank controls. A score lead alone in fair decisive first-to-five converts at163/256≈63.67%; do not use50% as the null for leader conversion. This benchmark is mathematical, not this game's measured probability.
3. Each EX buy vs no-buy player retaining the exact price, then two-EX mixed kits. Distinguish selection, legal activation, useful threat and counterplay; repeated use does not itself prove value.
4. Each skill category at threshold, cap and reflection/multihit/correction boundary. Reward-seeking vs normal winning policies, multiple seeds and multiple supported opponents.
5. Human pairs: tutorial-free shop comprehension, then trained adapted rematches; rotate seats/fighters/treatments. Record opponent habits that justified changes, perceived helplessness, actual pause/command errors and desire to rematch. Small samples are usability evidence, not tight balance confidence.

No unsourced win-rate claims, no prices justified solely by old direct-spend bots. Pre-register exploratory tolerances with the owner; report uncertainty by player-pair/seed blocks, not mirrored duplicates as independent trials. Do not force a trailer's total match chance to50%, which would erase earned score. Do not make no-buy always optimal either.
